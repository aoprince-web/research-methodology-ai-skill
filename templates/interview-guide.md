# Semi-Structured Interview Guide Template

Adapt this template to the research question, participants, setting, language, and ethics approval. It is a planning aid, not a substitute for piloting or supervisor review.

## Opening and consent

Use wording approved for the study. Confirm that the participant understands the study purpose, voluntary nature of participation, right to decline questions or withdraw within the stated limits, recording arrangements, confidentiality and its limits, data handling, and how to ask questions. Do not imply that confidentiality can be guaranteed where the research design or setting makes that impossible.

## Interview questions

| Topic or theme | Main open question | Neutral probes | Research question linked to | Notes after piloting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Context and background | Can you tell me a little about your experience of…? | Could you tell me more? What happened next? | | |
| Main experience or issue | How would you describe…? | Can you give an example from your experience? | | |
| Meaning or interpretation | What does this mean to you? | Has your view changed over time? | | |
| Factors or process | What do you think influenced this? | What else played a part? | | |
| Closing | Is there anything important that I have not asked about? | Is there anything you would like to clarify? | | |

Replace generic prompts with questions grounded in the study. Avoid leading formulations such as “How much did X affect you?” if the study has not established that X affected the participant.

## After the interview

- Record field notes and contextual observations separately from verbatim transcript material.
- Document changes to the guide and the reason for each change.
- Transfer recordings and notes to approved secure storage as soon as practicable.
- Follow the approved retention, access, and destruction plan.
- Keep analytic reflections distinct from participants' words.
- Do not include identifiable details in working notes unless necessary and approved.

## Pilot review

After piloting, check:
- Are the questions clear and understandable?
- Do they invite detailed answers without suggesting a preferred answer?
- Are sensitive questions necessary and appropriately placed?
- Is the interview length realistic?
- Are important areas missing or repeated?
- Can the questions produce evidence relevant to the research questions?
- Does the guide comply with the approved protocol?
