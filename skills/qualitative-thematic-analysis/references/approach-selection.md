# Selecting a qualitative analysis approach

This guide prevents the skill from treating all qualitative analysis as interchangeable.

## Start with the analytic purpose

| Research need | Possible approach | Key analytic product |
|---|---|---|
| Interpret patterned meanings across a dataset, with researcher reflexivity central | Braun and Clarke reflexive thematic analysis | Themes organised around central concepts |
| Apply a structured set of codes across a team or applied project | Codebook thematic analysis | Defined codebook and patterned findings |
| Compare cases, groups or stakeholders against applied questions | Framework analysis | Case-by-theme matrix and explanatory patterns |
| Categorise textual material systematically, with a defined unit and analytic orientation | Qualitative content analysis | Categories and, where justified, themes |

These are indicative fits, not automatic rules. Confirm the study's epistemological position, research question, design, supervisor expectations and intended claims.

## Reflexive thematic analysis

The researcher actively interprets patterned meaning. Coding and theme development are recursive and reflexive. The six phases are a flexible guide. A fixed codebook, consensus coding or inter-rater reliability should not be imposed as universal quality criteria.

## Codebook thematic analysis

A structured codebook is central. It should specify code labels, definitions, inclusion and exclusion guidance, and illustrative data segments. Explain how codes were developed, piloted, revised and applied. Team consistency procedures may be relevant when aligned with the chosen variant.

## Framework analysis

The framework method is particularly useful for applied questions and comparisons across cases. The matrix should preserve the link between each summary and its original transcript location. Summarisation must not erase nuance, context or contradictory evidence.

## Qualitative content analysis

Specify:
- conventional, directed or summative approach;
- manifest, latent or combined level;
- unit of analysis and meaning unit;
- condensation and abstraction procedures;
- coding and category development;
- whether counts are used and how they are interpreted.

Do not treat a word count as a complete analysis of meaning.

## Decision record

Record:
- chosen approach;
- reason for selection;
- approaches considered and why they were not selected;
- relevant theoretical or epistemological assumptions;
- implications for coding, theme development, quality and reporting;
- supervisor or institutional requirements.
