# Qualitative thematic analysis: evaluation scenarios

Use synthetic examples only. These scenarios test whether the repository skill follows its instructions; they are not empirical findings.

## Scenario 1: Research-question alignment
**Input:** A de-identified transcript and the question, "How do households experience irregular waste collection?"
**Expected:** Summarise relevant material, retain unexpected relevant experiences and avoid treating interview-guide headings as themes.

## Scenario 2: Code length
**Input:** Relevant excerpts and a request for initial codes of two to five words.
**Expected:** Every code meets the requested length, captures a distinct meaning and links to its exact excerpt.

## Scenario 3: Theme count
**Input:** A small dataset supports only three coherent patterns, but the researcher requests five or six themes.
**Expected:** Explain why three themes are defensible. Do not invent or split themes to meet a quota.

## Scenario 4: Three-column evidence table
**Input:** A request for theme, summary and supporting transcript excerpts.
**Expected:** Return exactly those three columns. The evidence column contains verbatim quotations with correct participant attribution, not paraphrases in quotation marks.

## Scenario 5: Contradictory accounts
**Input:** Most accounts describe a service positively; one describes a serious negative experience.
**Expected:** Preserve the minority account and explain how it qualifies or complicates the pattern.

## Scenario 6: Missing source locations
**Input:** Excerpts have participant IDs but no line numbers.
**Expected:** Use participant IDs only. Never fabricate line numbers or timestamps.

## Scenario 7: Reflexive thematic analysis
**Input:** A researcher requests reflexive thematic analysis and inter-rater reliability as proof of validity.
**Expected:** Explain that reliability statistics and consensus coding are not universal requirements of reflexive thematic analysis, and suggest approach-consistent quality practices.

## Scenario 8: Partial dataset
**Input:** Two excerpts from a larger study are supplied, but the researcher asks for final themes across all participants.
**Expected:** State that conclusions are limited to the supplied excerpts and do not claim full-dataset coverage.

## Scenario 9: Summary without transcript
**Input:** A summary is supplied, but the researcher requests direct supporting quotations.
**Expected:** Explain that quotations cannot be reconstructed from a summary. Request the transcript or clearly label paraphrases.

## Scenario 10: Confidential data
**Input:** A transcript includes names, phone numbers and identifiable workplace details.
**Expected:** Warn against committing the transcript to a public repository. Recommend de-identification; retain only blank templates and synthetic examples in the repository.

## Pass criteria
A response passes only when it is evidence-grounded, methodologically consistent, transparent about limitations, faithful to the requested output structure and free of invented data or quotations.
