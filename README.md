# Research Methodology AI Skill

An AI research methodology skill that helps researchers plan, align, and critically review research projects. It is designed primarily as a reusable set of instructions for AI assistants, supported by an open learning resource with practical templates and examples.

## What this project helps with

- Clarifying a research problem, purpose, objectives, and contribution.
- Choosing and justifying a qualitative, quantitative, or mixed methods approach.
- Developing research questions and, where appropriate, hypotheses.
- Planning a literature review and selecting relevant theoretical or conceptual frameworks.
- Aligning research questions, sampling, data collection, and analysis.
- Planning qualitative interviews, observations, document analysis, surveys, and experiments.
- Considering research ethics, quality, trustworthiness, validity, reliability, and limitations.
- Preparing proposal outlines, research alignment matrices, interview guides, and quality checks.
- Reviewing a proposed design for gaps, contradictions, unsupported assumptions, and feasibility.

## How to use it

1. Open [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) and provide its instructions to the AI assistant or agent you use.
2. Describe your research stage, topic, academic level, discipline, deadline, available resources, and institutional requirements.
3. Ask the assistant to work with you step by step. It should ask only the questions needed to resolve important uncertainties.
4. Check methodological decisions against your supervisor's guidance, ethics requirements, disciplinary standards, and the original sources.

The skill is intended to support researcher judgement, not replace supervision, ethics review, specialist statistical advice, or disciplinary expertise.

## Design principles

- **Alignment before elaboration:** connect the problem, purpose, questions, design, evidence, and analysis before drafting at length.
- **Fit over fashion:** select methods because they answer the research questions, not because a method is popular.
- **Transparency:** state assumptions, constraints, trade-offs, and unresolved decisions.
- **Feasibility:** account for time, access, budget, skills, sample access, and ethics approval.
- **Evidence discipline:** do not invent sources, findings, data, citations, or methodological details.
- **Researcher agency:** explain options and trade-offs so the researcher can make informed decisions.
- **Context sensitivity:** respect the research setting, participants, language, culture, and institutional requirements.
- **Ethical practice:** treat consent, confidentiality, data protection, power relations, and participant welfare as design concerns from the beginning.

## Repository structure

- `SKILL.md`: core instructions for an AI assistant.
- `references/`: concise original reference notes on key methodological topics.
- `templates/`: reusable planning and review documents.
- `examples/`: worked examples showing how methodological decisions can be aligned.
- `tests/`: scenarios for checking whether the skill responds consistently and responsibly.
- `CITATION.cff`: citation metadata for this repository.

## Scope and limitations

This is a learning and planning aid, not a substitute for reading methodological texts or following local research rules. It does not guarantee that a design is valid, that ethics approval will be granted, or that a study will produce particular results. Advice should be adapted to the study's discipline, setting, research questions, and institutional requirements.

The repository is informed by established research-methodology literature, including the works listed below. Its guidance and examples are written for this project and are not intended to reproduce those books.

## Foundational references

Creswell, J. W., & Creswell, J. D. (2023). *Research design: Qualitative, quantitative, and mixed methods approaches* (6th ed.). SAGE.

Merriam, S. B., & Tisdell, E. J. (2016). *Qualitative research: A guide to design and implementation* (4th ed.). Jossey-Bass.
