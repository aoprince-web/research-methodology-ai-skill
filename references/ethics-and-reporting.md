# Research Ethics and Reporting

Ethics, data management, and reporting should be planned alongside the design. The exact rules depend on the institution, jurisdiction, discipline, funder, and study.

## Ethical planning

### Consent and voluntariness

Explain the study in accessible language, including its purpose, procedures, foreseeable risks, expected benefits where applicable, data use, confidentiality and its limits, and the participant's choices. Participation should be voluntary. Consider whether a gatekeeper, employer, teacher, health professional, or family member could exert pressure.

### Privacy and confidentiality

Distinguish anonymity from confidentiality. Data may be confidential but identifiable to the research team. Small communities, unusual roles, quotations, or combinations of details may allow re-identification even after names are removed. Consider these risks in collection, storage, analysis, and publication.

### Data management

Set out what data will be collected, who can access it, where and how it will be stored, how identifiers will be separated or protected, how long records will be retained, and how they will be destroyed or archived. Follow the approved plan and applicable rules.

### Risk and participant welfare

Consider emotional discomfort, stigma, reputational harm, employment consequences, disclosure of sensitive information, and other context-specific risks. Explain how the study will respond if distress or an unexpected ethical issue arises.

### Permissions and approval

Identify institutional ethics review, site permissions, data-owner permission, and other approvals required before relevant research activities begin. State approval status accurately. A proposal must not say that approval has been granted if the application is still planned or pending.

## Reporting integrity

- Report what was actually done, not only what was planned.
- Distinguish planned, ongoing, and completed procedures.
- Do not invent data, participant quotations, analysis steps, approvals, or references.
- Describe changes to the design and explain material deviations.
- Report limitations and contradictory evidence honestly.
- Protect participant identity in quotations, case descriptions, tables, and appendices.
- Follow the applicable reporting guidance for the design and discipline where required.

## Responsible use of AI in research

Researchers should follow institutional, publisher, funder, and ethics requirements for AI use. AI can assist with planning, language editing, coding support, or organisation, but it should not be treated as a source of verified evidence by default.

Verify factual claims and citations independently. Do not upload identifiable or confidential research data to an AI service unless the data use is permitted and the service is appropriate. Disclose AI assistance when required. The researcher remains responsible for the accuracy, integrity, and ethical conduct of the work.

## Final questions

- Have relevant approvals and permissions been identified?
- Is consent appropriate for the participants and setting?
- Are risks and safeguards specific to this study?
- Could people be identified from the reported details?
- Is data storage and retention consistent with policy and approval?
- Are all reported methods, findings, and citations accurate?
